% ------------------------------------------------------------------ Slide 1: the arc
\begin{frame}{The arc --- profiling reshaped the plan}
\footnotesize
Last time: a fused DeepSeek-R1 MoE region (\empr{1.56$\times$ prefill}) $+$ a \emph{TileComm} traffic-shaping idea. This time I profiled the two candidate communication abstractions \empy{directly on 8$\times$MI350X} --- and the measurement changed the plan.
\vspace{1ex}
\begin{columns}
\begin{column}{0.32\textwidth}
\examplebox{What died}{\footnotesize \empr{Traffic-shaping.} Reorder-only comm scheduling is capped at \empy{1--4\%} over a good round-robin on a fully-connected fabric; the on-node probe was issue-bound.}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.34\textwidth}
\examplebox{The two real levers}{\footnotesize \empy{(1) decode weight wall} $\to$ fewer weight bytes (MXFP4 / a QuantTile). \empy{(2) TP4 prefill all-reduce} $=$ 60--85\% of GEMM+AR $\to$ tile-fused overlap, real 1.4--1.7$\times$ ceiling.}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.34\textwidth}
\examplebox{The unifier}{\footnotesize A \empr{typed tile edge} (\mc{stage}/\mc{retire}): a tile whose \emph{residency} and \emph{format} are first-class. \empy{QuantTile-v0} is the falsifiable first milestone.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\vspace{1ex}
\centering\empr{The abstraction is the same; the profiling told me which lowering to build first --- and it is not the comm one.}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ Slide 2: recap
\begin{frame}{Recap --- the fused region works, and it told me where to look}
\footnotesize
\begin{columns}
\begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item \empr{Prefill: 1.56$\times$} over the unfused MORI-dispatch $\to$ aiter-fmoe $\to$ MORI-combine baseline (1247 vs 1941\,$\mu$s), correctness-gated (RMS 0.018).
  \item We fuse the expert \emph{region}: the gather \empy{produces the GEMM's packed layout}, so \empy{7 kernels collapse to 3} --- no sort, no standalone quant, no scatter-combine. HipKittens compute $+$ IRIS comm.
  \item \empr{Decode is only marginal} ($\sim$2--3\%, 516.5 vs 527\,$\mu$s at matched fp8) --- because decode is a \empy{weight-memory wall} (next). Fusion cannot move the dominant cost there.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.45\textwidth}
\examplebox{Two regimes, two bottlenecks}{\footnotesize
\empy{Prefill} is token-scaled compute followed by a \empr{TP4 all-reduce}. \empy{Decode} streams \empr{all 32 local experts' weights} every step with \emph{no} TP all-reduce (DP-attention).
\vspace{1ex}\\
So the levers are \emph{different per regime} --- which is exactly what the new profiling had to pin down before committing to an abstraction.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ Slide 3: the pivot
\begin{frame}{The pivot --- traffic-shaping is a weak lever (measured, honest)}
\footnotesize
The seed: the combine's \empr{2.4$\times$} (934$\to$386\,$\mu$s) from \emph{one} hand-written line --- \mc{build\_combine\_pull(interleave=True)} round-robins destination cells across the 8 XGMI links (sorted-by-dst piles concurrent blocks onto one link; round-robin spreads them). I tried to generalize it into a demand-aware scheduler. It doesn't:
\vspace{0.5ex}
\begin{columns}
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\centering\scriptsize cost model (\mc{tilesched.py}, calibrated to 934/386)
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
schedule & model $\mu$s & vs naive \\
\midrule
\empr{sorted} (naive default) & 927.8 & 1.0$\times$ \\
round-robin (today) & 388.2 & \empy{2.4$\times$} \\
\empy{proportional} (byte-aware) & 388.3 & (= rr) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\vspace{0.5ex}\\
\scriptsize demand-aware beats round-robin by only \empr{1--4\%}, growing slowly with expert skew.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
\examplebox{Why it's capped}{\scriptsize
On a \empr{fully-connected} 8-GPU XGMI fabric, \emph{bytes-per-link are fixed by the demand matrix.} Reordering \emph{when} blocks fire only avoids the pathological sorted order --- it \empy{cannot beat the hottest-link floor}.}
\vspace{-1ex}
\examplebox{And the probe was inconclusive}{\scriptsize
The on-node XGMI probe was \empr{issue-bound} (fire-and-forget stores timed \emph{issue rate}: $\sim$4.7\,TB/s, $>$10$\times$ the fabric) and showed $\sim$1.05$\times$. \empy{Inconclusive, not a refutation} --- not a lever to bet on.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\centering\empr{So I stopped tuning the order and re-profiled the two candidate comm abstractions directly on-node.}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ Slide 4: methodology
\begin{frame}{Profiling methodology --- exactly what was measured}
\footnotesize
\begin{columns}
\begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item \empy{Node:} the dedicated 8$\times$MI350X (gfx950), container \mc{subha\_probe} (torch 2.10$+$rocm7.2, triton 3.6, IRIS editable). Config \empr{TP4}, \empr{bf16}.
  \item \empy{Unfused reference $=$ what production does today:} a full-256-CU \emph{torch} matmul of each rank's \empr{K-slice partial GEMM}, \emph{then} a separate \empr{RCCL all-reduce}. Two ops, not fused.
  \item \empy{Timing:} \empr{median-of-30} CUDA-event, then \empr{MAX over the 4 ranks} (Simran's rule: long all-reduce times are usually profiling artifacts --- use in-kernel / max-over-ranks).
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\examplebox{Each shape is a real R1 op}{\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}\scriptsize
  \item \mc{8192,7168,18432} $=$ R1 \empy{down-projection} (prefill).
  \item \mc{8192,7168,7168} $=$ R1 \empy{attention out-projection} (prefill).
  \item \mc{1024/256,7168,18432} $=$ small-$M$ / decode-ish $M$ (the same op at lower token count).
\end{itemize}
\empr{No synthetic shapes} --- every $(M,N,K)$ is an all-reduce-preceding GEMM on the TP4 prefill path.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\vspace{0.5ex}
\centering\empy{Question the profile answers:} is the TP4 all-reduce cheap-relative-to-GEMM (then comm fusion is pointless) or does it dominate?
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ Slide 7: decode regime
\begin{frame}{The other regime --- decode is weight-bound, with no all-reduce to fuse}
\footnotesize
\begin{columns}
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item Under \empy{DP-attention} the TP all-reduce is \empr{gone at decode} --- there is nothing for abstraction A to fuse.
  \item The expert GEMM streams \empr{all 32 local experts' $\sim$1.4\,GB of fp8 weights every step} --- a fixed \empr{$\sim$176\,$\mu$s HBM floor} at 8\,TB/s, independent of token count. Gather$+$combine $\approx$ \empy{18\%} of the region.
  \item So \empy{decode $\approx$ 80\% weight floor $+$ 20\% boundaries.} \emph{Any} comm fusion caps decode at $\sim$1.1--1.25$\times$.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\examplebox{The only high-ceiling decode lever}{\footnotesize
Move \empr{fewer weight bytes.} That is a \emph{representation} lever, not a comm/overlap one --- and it is where serving latency is actually set.
\vspace{1ex}\\
$\Rightarrow$ \empy{MXFP4 weights} ($\frac14$ the bf16 bytes) directly attack the 80\% floor. This is abstraction \empy{B (QuantTile)}, and it is the higher-serving-impact of the two.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\vspace{0.3ex}
\centering\empr{A (comm) $=$ prefill throughput; B (format) $=$ decode latency. They attack different regimes --- and B owns the serving denominator.}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ Slide 9: the abstraction
\begin{frame}[fragile]{The abstraction --- a typed tile edge (\mc{stage} / \mc{retire})}
\footnotesize
A tile whose \empr{RESIDENCY} (may live on another rank) and \empr{FORMAT} (may be compressed) are \empy{first-class type parameters}. The author declares an edge; the compiler lowers each axis to its own backend.
\begin{columns}
\begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
\begin{lstlisting}
// inbound: fill an HK tile (QuantTile = FORMAT)
stage(b_tile)
  .from( local() )                   // RESIDENCY
  .format( mx_fp4{scale,32,E8M0} )   // FORMAT
  .for_mma<Bt>();

// outbound: dispose a tile (Overlap-A)
retire(c)
  .reduce_scatter( tp_group, sum )   // RESIDENCY
  .overlap_with( next );             // SCHEDULE
\end{lstlisting}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\examplebox{Two lowerings, one declaration}{\scriptsize
\empy{QuantTile} (FORMAT, decode) $\to$ CDNA4 scaled-MFMA / in-register dequant. \empy{Tile-fused reduce-scatter} (RESIDENCY, prefill) $\to$ IRIS RMA $+$ a tile schedule. Same two verbs; the compiler dispatches on the type axes.}
\vspace{-1ex}
\examplebox{Honest boundary}{\scriptsize
An \empr{API-level unifier, not one cost model.} The axes are orthogonal (residency $\neq$ numerics, format $\neq$ topology), so each keeps its \emph{own} validated model --- by design. No scalar objective spans $\mu$s-of-comm vs HBM-bytes vs RMS.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup --- anticipated questions}
\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item \empr{``Why abandon the comm abstraction Awad asked for?''} I didn't --- I re-pointed it. Traffic-shaping (the \emph{scheduling} half) is capped at 1--4\%. The comm lever that \emph{is} real is \empy{fusion} (Overlap-A, tile-fused reduce-scatter) on the TP4 prefill AR --- kept as the second lowering of the same \mc{stage}/\mc{retire} notation, with a measured 1.4--1.7$\times$ ceiling and an honest build cost.
  \item \empr{``Isn't `residency $+$ format' just one big cost model you don't have?''} Correct that we don't have it --- and we deliberately don't claim it. The axes are \empy{orthogonal} (residency doesn't change numerics; format doesn't change topology), so each lowers to its \emph{own} validated model. The word ``unify'' applies to the \empy{declaration surface}, never to the objective function.
  \item \empr{``Did you get a new end-to-end MXFP4 speedup?''} No --- deliberately. Route-1 is shipped ($\sim$1.6$\times$ GEMM, 1.11$\times$ region); the missing piece is a \empy{legitimate external baseline} (the SGLang R1-FP4 e2e), because the a4w4 kernel we captured was untuned/buggy. Never iterate against a kernel we captured ourselves.
  \item \empr{``Why QuantTile before the combine or Overlap-A?''} It attacks the \emph{dominant} decode cost (the 80\% weight floor), it is falsifiable against trusted numbers (G1--G4), and it needs \emph{no} new GEMM body --- the two fast decode kernels already exist; v0 makes them \empy{one} at zero cost.
\end{itemize}
